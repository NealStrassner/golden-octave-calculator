note ring The Cosine Ruler — the sliding line, d = 2R·cos(6k°) the two crossings the pentagram — locks at k = 6 and 12
The bright yellow line between the two centres is the cosine ruler — the measuring instrument from the companion paper The Cosine Ruler and the Selection Law. Its length is the distance d = 2R·cos(6k°), and it grows and shrinks as you turn the dial.
The faint five-pointed star is inscribed in the note ring, riding the upper crossing. It catches the lower crossing only when 12k is a multiple of 72 — that is, only at k = 6 and k = 12, where it locks, turns gold, and the two voices fuse to an octave: the crossings sit on the pentagon's own vertices, d = R·φ and d = R/φ, and the two golden distances together make R·φ + R/φ = R·√5.
Press Start Tone, then drag the dial while it plays — the two voices change live. The A4 = 432 Hz switch retunes the whole ring: the A-family becomes whole numbers (108 · 216 · 432 · 864 Hz), while the other equal-tempered notes stay irrational — whole-number scales belong to just intonation (The Just Ring, forthcoming).
Every value on this dial, and the drawing itself, is computed with Neal’s Net trig system, from the ring’s own anchors, not the browser’s sine and cosine.
